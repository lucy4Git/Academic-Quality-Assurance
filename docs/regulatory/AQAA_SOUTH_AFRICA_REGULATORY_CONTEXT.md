## The South African NQF Hierarchy

The National Qualifications Framework (NQF) is governed at multiple levels:

```
DHET (Department of Higher Education and Training)
  — policy and legislative mandate

SAQA (South African Qualifications Authority)
  — NQF oversight and qualification registration

Quality Councils:
  CHE (Council on Higher Education)  — HE sector (NQF 5–10)
  QCTO (Quality Council for Trades and Occupations)  — Trades/occupational (NQF 1–6)
  Umalusi  — General and Further Education (NQF 1–4)

Professional Councils (sector-specific accreditation):
  ECSA  — Engineering professions
  HPCSA  — Health professions
  SACE  — Education profession
  (and many others)
```

## Key Regulatory Instruments

| Instrument | Issuing body | Relevance |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| Higher Education Act (1997) | Parliament | Legal basis for HE governance |
| NQF Act (2008) | Parliament | Legal basis for NQF |
| HEQSF (2013) | DHET/CHE | Qualification standards and descriptors |
| Criteria for Programme Accreditation (2004, updated) | CHE | Programme-level accreditation requirements |
| ECSA Accreditation Criteria | ECSA | Engineering programme accreditation |
| HPCSA Accreditation Standards | HPCSA | Health professions accreditation |
| SACE Professional Standards | SACE | Teacher education programme standards |
| QCTO Occupational Qualifications Framework | QCTO | Occupational qualification requirements |

## Dual-Framework Compliance

Many South African programmes must comply with BOTH:
1. **CHE/HEQSF** — general quality assurance for all HE programmes
2. **Professional body** (ECSA, HPCSA, SACE, etc.) — sector-specific accreditation

This creates multi-framework assessment scenarios where:
- Evidence submitted for CHE may satisfy some ECSA criteria (cross-framework overlap)
- Some ECSA requirements may conflict with CHE's interpretation (cross-framework conflict)
- Evidence packs for submission to ECSA must also satisfy CHE documentation requirements

AQAA's cross-framework mapping engine handles these scenarios.

## What AQAA Does NOT Do

AQAA is a Quality Assurance **management and audit platform**, not a legal compliance tool.

It does NOT:
- Provide legal advice
- Guarantee accreditation outcomes
- Replace the role of the CHE, SAQA, or any professional body
- Auto-verify that imported regulatory content is current or complete
- Represent official regulatory requirements (all content is `[TEST FIXTURE]` in development)

Always consult the relevant authority's official website and publications for authoritative requirements.
